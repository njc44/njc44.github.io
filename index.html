Vision

I want a world where autonomous physical agents take on much of the hard, informal, unorganised labour, so people get their time back for work that means something to them.

I believe in the dignity of labour, for everyone. That means the move to physical AI has to be designed around the people who do this work today. Automating it in the wrong economy at the wrong time takes away livelihoods.

A good guide is the Lewis turning point. That's the moment an economy runs out of surplus rural labour and wages start rising quickly. Where an economy sits on that curve should decide what physical AI does there.

Below the turning point

Countries that still have surplus labour are where physical AI should gather its training data: teleoperation, demonstrations and annotation. That work pays people and builds skills without displacing anyone.

Past the turning point

Countries with shrinking or ageing workforces are where autonomous systems should be deployed, filling gaps that people are leaving anyway.

Done in that order, physical AI adds to economies instead of hollowing them out.

Further out: quantum

I expect quantum AI applications to become practical in the 2040s. I've been reading about quantum algorithms and completed IBM's quantum computing course, so I'm ready when the hardware catches up.
